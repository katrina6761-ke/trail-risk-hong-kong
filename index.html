01 / BACKGROUND

Why assess hiking risk by trail segment?

Hong Kong attracts millions of visitors to hike each year, while risks can vary between sections of the same trail. This study asks how to build a scientific indicator system, assess section-level risk, and present results clearly for different users.

03 / DATA

A territory-wide trail network and multi-source GIS data

The proposal defines the study area as hiking trails in Hong Kong’s country parks. It combines terrain, habitat, hazard, access and emergency-support data for section-level analysis.

TERRAIN & HAZARDS

Physical environment

Digital Terrain Model (raster) supports slope analysis; the Territory-wide Terrestrial Habitat Map supports land-cover classification; boulder points and historical landslide catchments support proximity and overlay analysis.

ROUTES & ACCESS

Network and nearby facilities

AFCD Hiking Trails in Country Parks define the route network. Road networks, trail distance posts, fire stations, shelters, refreshment kiosks and public water-filling stations support proximity, spacing and accessibility measures.

CONNECTIVITY & PROVENANCE

Signal data and coordinate handling

The proposal identifies OFCA mobile-coverage maps and CSDI as source channels. The supplied route file uses EPSG:2326 and is transformed in the browser. Dataset-specific download or acquisition dates are not recorded in the proposal or current GeoJSON.

04 / METHOD

A multi-criteria assessment workflow

The proposal describes a planned workflow that combines expert judgement, indicator data and GIS analysis. The website displays the supplied results; it does not recalculate them.

01

Define indicators and score segments

Review literature to select natural, social and human factors, then use GIS overlay and proximity analysis to derive scores for each trail section.

02

Combine subjective and objective weights

The proposal uses AHP pairwise comparisons for expert-informed weights and plans to introduce entropy weights from data variation as the full dataset becomes available.

03

Map and classify the results

The proposal specifies ArcGIS Pro 3.0.1 for spatial processing, followed by an interactive Web GIS view for hikers and managers.

CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

Drawing on accident-system and human-factors perspectives, the proposal treats safety as an interaction among environmental hazards, trail infrastructure, hiker exposure and emergency response. Hikers’ fitness, experience and risk awareness can shape how they respond to similar conditions; this prototype maps segment attributes and does not personalize a score to an individual.

05 / ADVICE

Use risk classes to guide decisions

Map classes are planning information, not a live safety assessment. Always check official alerts, weather and current trail conditions before setting out.

LOW · 低

Prepare as usual

Check the forecast, carry water and a charged phone, and share your itinerary.

MODERATE · 中

Allow extra time

Use suitable footwear, plan rest points and keep to marked trails.

HIGH · 高

Review conditions carefully

Consider your group’s ability, avoid deteriorating conditions and be ready to turn back.

VERY HIGH · 很高

Consider an alternative

Check for closures and official warnings; defer or change plans if conditions or preparation are unsuitable.

FOR TRAIL MANAGERS

Use higher-class segments to prioritize field inspection, maintenance review, wayfinding and communication coverage checks. Confirm findings on site before making operational or closure decisions.
